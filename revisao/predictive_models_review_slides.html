Model 1

Logistic Regression: a probability gate

Classification

It combines evidence and uses the sigmoid curve to turn the result into a probability between 0 and 1.

  1. 1Combines features with weights.
  2. 2Applies the sigmoid function.
  3. 3Compares against a threshold, usually 0.5.
  4. 4Returns a probability and a class.
AnalogyA gate that gradually opens as the evidence grows.
Model ReviewSimple, fast, and interpretable

Model 4

KNN: ask the nearby neighbors

Classification and regression

To predict a new example, KNN consults the K most similar examples and combines their answers.

  1. 1Computes distance to the known data.
  2. 2Selects the K nearest neighbors.
  3. 3Uses voting for classification or an average for regression.
Small KSensitive to noise.
Large KMay oversmooth.

Essential: standardize features, because distance depends on scale.

Model 5

Naive Bayes: update a belief with evidence

Probabilistic

It starts with an initial probability and asks: how likely are these clues in each class?

  1. 1Defines the initial chance of each class — the prior.
  2. 2Measures the strength of the evidence — the likelihood.
  3. 3Updates the probabilities and chooses the largest posterior.
Why “naive”?It assumes features are independent within each class. This is a simplification, but it works very well for many text tasks.

Fast and efficient for spam, sentiment, and document classification.

Example: is this email spam?

Model ReviewEvery metric begins with these four numbers

Metrics

A lens for each question

Accuracy

(TP + TN) / all

How many predictions were correct overall?

Useful with balanced classes.

Precision

TP / (TP + FP)

Of the alerts, how many were correct?

Controls false alarms.

Recall

TP / (TP + FN)

Of the actual positives, how many did we find?

Controls missed cases.

F1

harmonic mean of P and R

How can we balance Precision and Recall?

Useful with class imbalance.

ROC-AUC

0.5 → 1.0

Does the model rank positives above negatives?

Evaluates many thresholds.

Accuracy can misleadIf 99% of transactions are normal, always predicting “normal” gives 99% accuracy while detecting zero fraud.

Generalization

Overfitting: when the model memorizes

The silent enemy

“I memorized the answer sheet, but the test brought new questions.”

UnderfittingModel is too simple: poor on both training and validation.
Good generalizationLearns the pattern and works on new data.
OverfittingExcellent training score, worse validation score: it learned noise and details.

Countermeasures: more data, cross-validation, regularization, fewer features, a smaller tree, and a simple baseline.

Model ReviewGoal: generalize, do not memorize

Best practices

How do we choose and improve a model?

1 · BaselineStart with a rule or simple model.
2 · Fair comparisonSame split, features, and metric.
3 · Cross-validationObserve the mean and variation across folds.
4 · HyperparametersTune through search without looking at the test set.
5 · ErrorsInvestigate where and for whom the model fails.

Between clustersGroups should be different.

Model ReviewWithout an answer key: structure emerges from the data

Clustering

K-Means: bring each point closer to a center

You must choose K

Imagine placing K meeting points to minimize everyone’s travel distance.

  1. 1Choose K initial centroids.
  2. 2Assign each point to its nearest center.
  3. 3Move each center to the group mean.
  4. 4Repeat until stable.
InertiaSum of squared distances to centroids. Lower means more compact groups.

Feature scaling is essential: a large scale may dominate distance.

Model ReviewInitialization can change the result

Choosing K

Number of groups: use two sources of evidence

ElbowInertia always decreases; we look for the curve’s “elbow.”
Silhouette: −1 to +1Near 1 means well grouped; 0 means a boundary; negative suggests a poor assignment.

Beyond K-Means

Group shape and the meaning of distance matter

Hierarchical

Builds a tree of groups. The dendrogram shows the distance at which groups merge.

DBSCAN

Finds dense regions, irregular shapes, and noise points. It uses eps and minPts.

Distances

Euclidean: straight line. Manhattan: city blocks. Cosine: direction. Jaccard: overlap.
